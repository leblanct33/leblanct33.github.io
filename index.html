Fill Up in Edmonton?

Gas prices, Edmonton vs Calgary, 2019–2026: should a fleet refuel in Edmonton?

MacEwan University · Intro to Business Statistics · 94 months of StatCan data

Answer

Yes, when the route already passes through Edmonton. Historically, Edmonton regular gas averaged 3.7¢/L cheaper than Calgary (95% CI 2.7 to 4.7¢, adjusted for month-to-month correlation), and was cheaper in 90 of 94 months (historical frequency, not a guarantee).

1 · Requirements (AI-drafted, group-confirmed)

One question, for one decision-maker.

Should an Alberta business running vehicles between Edmonton and Calgary (a courier or trades company) do its refuelling in Edmonton?

Decision-maker

Fleet / operations manager.

Success criteria

  • Quantify the price gap
  • Test whether it is statistically real
  • Check seasonality
  • Give a dollar recommendation
  • Worthwhile only if net saving (after fuel, driver time and admin) is positive; never via detours

Scope

Monthly regular unleaded, self-service, January 2019 to October 2026.

Regular gasoline vehicles only (not diesel).

Deliverables

  • This published report
  • Dataset and data dictionary
  • AI review report and corrections log
  • Slides (see Project documents)

9 · Analysis (MGTS-103 methods)

The gap is small, but it is real.

01

Design

Paired-sample design

Each month gives one price in each city, so we analyse the 94 monthly differences d = Calgary − Edmonton.

d̄ = 3.73¢ · s_d = 2.34 · n = 94

SE = s_d / √n = 2.34 / √94 = 0.241

02

Estimate

95% confidence interval

d̄ ± t(0.025, 93) × SE
= 3.73 ± 1.986 × 0.241
= (3.25, 4.20) ¢/L

We are 95% confident the true average gap is between 3.25 and 4.20 cents per litre.

Autocorrelation-adjusted: 2.71 to 4.74 ¢/L (see Check).

03

Test

Paired t-test

H0: μd = 0 · H1: μd > 0

α = 0.05; reject H0 if p < 0.05.

t = d̄ / SE = 3.73 / 0.241 = 15.47 · df = 93 · p < 0.0001

Reject H0: Calgary is significantly more expensive than Edmonton.

Decision
Reject H0

04

Check

Check: are months independent?

Monthly gaps are autocorrelated (lag-1 r = 0.61), so the 94 months carry about as much information as 23 independent ones.

Adjusted: effective n ≈ 22.9 · SE = 0.488 · t ≈ 7.63 · 95% CI (2.71, 4.74) ¢/L · p < 0.0001

Conclusion unchanged: reject H0.

Decision
Reject H0

05

Proportion

How often Edmonton wins

p̂ = 90 / 94 = 95.7% of months

06

Relationship

Correlation and regression

Calgary = 3.96 + 0.998 × Edmonton · r = 0.995 · R² = 0.991

The slope CI (0.978–1.018) includes 1, consistent with a gap that does not grow as prices rise (Chart E).

07

Interpretation

Small, but very consistent

The city gap is small but very consistent. Seasonality is much larger (about 16¢, July vs December, 2019–2025), but a business can't easily shift when it drives.

10 · Recommendations

Fill in Edmonton when it's on the route; budget by season.

Refuel in Edmonton

Before Calgary runs, when the route already passes through. Historically ~3.7¢/L cheaper (adjusted CI 2.7–4.7¢); check current prices.

Budget seasonally

Use historical averages (summer about 15¢/L above December–January) as a starting budget assumption, not a guarantee.

Don't detour to save

The gap is small, so fill in Edmonton only when it is already on the route.

A 10 km detour burns ~1.2 L (~$1.56 at $1.30/L), about what a 40 L fill saves, before driver time.

11 · Limitations

What this data cannot tell a fleet manager.

Averages only

Monthly city averages, not station-level or daily prices.

Discounts

Fleet card discounts and individual station pricing can beat the average gap.

Policy changes

The past gap may change with taxes or market events, e.g. the 2022 provincial fuel tax relief and the 2025 removal of the consumer carbon price, which affected both cities.

Assumed fleet

The dollar estimate uses assumed fleet numbers.

Regular gas only

Results do not apply to diesel or other fuels.

Recent trend

The last 12 months averaged a 3.8¢ gap and the last 24 months 3.5¢, close to the full-period 3.7¢, with Edmonton cheaper in all 24.
